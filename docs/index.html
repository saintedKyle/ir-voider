IR Voider

The offline IR remote for Android phones with a built-in IR blaster — or a DIY one on the headset jack.

Needs an IR emitter — built-in, or two IR LEDs wired back-to-back on the 3.5mm jack. It does not work on iPhones, iPads, tablets, or PCs, and no app can add IR hardware to a phone that has neither. Google Play release is pending.

DIY jack blaster

No built-in IR? The app plays each code as a 19 kHz tone out of the headset port for two back-to-back IR LEDs and a resistor. About 1–2 m, and the status chip tells you which emitter it measured.

How it works

The phone's IR LED sends the same kind of invisible pulses as a normal remote. IR Voider uses Android's ConsumerIrManager to transmit stored timing patterns. There is no Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, account or server involved. IR is line-of-sight: point the top edge at the device.

The phone cannot receive IR, so it cannot learn directly from another remote. Importing a raw capture made with an external IR receiver is supported.

On a phone with no IR blaster, the 3.5mm headset port is the one workable fallback. The app synthesises the code as a 19 kHz tone; two IR LEDs wired back-to-back each conduct on the half-cycle the other blocks, so together they pulse at 38 kHz — the carrier a TV receiver expects. A phone's audio output cannot produce 38 kHz by itself, so the doubling happens in the hardware, which is why one LED does not work. The app can measure that a plug is present and where the audio system routed the sound; it cannot prove light left an LED, because nothing here receives IR.
